## Summary

Published in 1963 (exact date uncertain; year only), "The Country, the Revolution and I" was Park Chung-hee's manifesto justifying the 1961 military coup and laying out his program for national reconstruction. In it he argued that poverty and weakness, not just communism, were Korea's existential threats; that a disciplined, state-directed national effort was needed to break the cycle of dependence; and that economic construction was the true continuation of the revolution. The First Five-Year Economic Development Plan (1962-1966) turned this vision into policy: export promotion, directed credit, and government-picked industrial priorities.

## Political Significance

- Provided the ideological charter for South Korea's developmental state: growth as the measure of legitimacy
- Established the "economy first" sequencing — development before democratization — that defined Park's rule
- Anchored the export-led model that took Korea from one of the world's poorest economies to an industrial power
- Set up the logic later used to justify the authoritarian Yushin Constitution (1972): security and development require centralized power

## Decision Rules Derived from This Source

- National survival depends on economic strength; every policy is judged by its contribution to growth and exports
- The state must lead development — directing credit, choosing industries, and holding firms to export targets
- Poverty and dependence are security threats as grave as military ones; self-reliance (in food, industry, defense) is a strategic goal
- Political order and discipline are preconditions for development, not obstacles to it
