## Original Excerpt

> "The proportion of planning to market forces is not the essential difference between socialism and capitalism. A planned economy is not equivalent to socialism, because there is planning under capitalism, too; a market economy is not capitalism, because there are markets under socialism, too. Planning and market forces are both means of controlling economic activity."

## Summary

At age 87, Deng Xiaoping traveled to Wuchang, Shenzhen, Zhuhai and Shanghai between January 18 and February 21, 1992, delivering a series of informal talks urging faster reform and opening-up. The talks were a response to the conservative backlash and reform stagnation that followed 1989, and they relaunched China's market-oriented transformation, paving the way for the "socialist market economy" adopted at the 14th Party Congress later that year.

## Political Significance

- Broke the ideological deadlock over whether markets were "capitalist" by redefining planning and markets as neutral instruments
- Legitimized special economic zones and coastal opening after years of political uncertainty
- Established development — not class struggle — as the criterion for judging the Party's work
- Demonstrated Deng's method of changing national policy through personal intervention rather than formal office (he held no major post at the time)

## Key Quotes for Agent Reasoning

- "Development is the hard truth." (发展才是硬道理)
- "It doesn't matter whether a cat is black or white, as long as it catches mice." (不管黑猫白猫，捉到老鼠就是好猫) — a Sichuan proverb Deng had quoted since the early 1960s to express pragmatism
- "Planning and market forces are both means of controlling economic activity."

## Decision Rules Derived from This Source

- Do not ask whether a policy is "socialist" or "capitalist"; ask whether it develops productive forces, strengthens the country, and raises living standards
- When conservatives block reform, demonstrate success experimentally (SEZs, coastal cities) and let results argue the case
- Move fast on economic opening while keeping political control firm
