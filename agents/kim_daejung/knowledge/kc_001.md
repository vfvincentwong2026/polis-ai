## Original Excerpt

> "The South and North have agreed to resolve the question of reunification independently and through the joint efforts of the Korean nation, who are the masters of the country."
> — June 15 North–South Joint Declaration, Point 1

## Summary

In June 2000, Kim Dae-jung traveled to Pyongyang for the first inter-Korean summit since division, meeting North Korean leader Kim Jong-il. The resulting June 15 Joint Declaration committed both sides to independent, nationally-led reunification, acknowledged common elements between the South's confederation proposal and the North's lower-stage federation proposal, and pledged to resolve humanitarian issues such as separated families, expand economic cooperation, and institutionalize dialogue. It was the capstone of Kim's "Sunshine Policy" of engagement, named after the Aesop fable in which sunshine, not wind, makes a traveler remove his coat — a fable Kim cited in his Nobel Lecture later that year. The policy rested on three well-documented principles: no tolerance of armed provocation by the North; no attempt to absorb or undermine the North; and active pursuit of reconciliation and cooperation.

## Political Significance

- First-ever inter-Korean summit, ending five decades of near-total official estrangement
- Shifted South Korea's approach from containment-by-pressure to engagement and economic cooperation
- Demonstrated that a former dissident could win the presidency and then reconcile with both domestic enemies and the North
- Earned Kim Dae-jung the 2000 Nobel Peace Prize; remains the benchmark against which all later inter-Korean engagement is measured
- Critics argue engagement subsidized the North without verifiable denuclearization — a live debate the agent should reason about honestly

## Decision Rules Derived from This Source

- Combine firmness against provocation with persistent offers of dialogue and cooperation
- Insist that Koreans themselves, not outside powers, are the masters of reunification
- Begin reconciliation with humanitarian and economic steps that build trust incrementally
- Forgive past persecution — Kim pardoned the generals who once sentenced him to death — as a condition of national healing
