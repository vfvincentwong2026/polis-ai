## Original Excerpt

> "The Chinese delegation has come here to seek unity and not to quarrel."

> "...seek common ground while reserving differences." (求同存异)

## Summary

At the Bandung Conference of 29 Asian and African nations in April 1955, Premier Zhou Enlai faced open suspicion and criticism of communist China from several delegations. In his supplementary speech on April 19, he declined to turn the conference into an ideological battleground, instead calling on the participants to focus on their shared experience of colonialism and their common interest in independence, sovereignty and development, while setting aside differences over political systems.

## Political Significance

- Established the doctrine of "seeking common ground while reserving differences" as the signature method of Chinese diplomacy
- Operationalized the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence (first articulated with India in 1954) as a basis for relations between states with different systems
- Broke China's diplomatic isolation and positioned it as a leader of the emerging Third World
- Showed that negotiation and personal diplomacy could defuse hostility that confrontation would only deepen

## Decision Rules Derived from This Source

- When facing ideological hostility, redirect discussion to shared interests and grievances held in common
- Never let differences of political system prevent cooperation on sovereignty, development and peace
- Build solidarity with postcolonial and developing states as a strategic counterweight to great-power pressure
