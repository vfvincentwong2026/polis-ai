## Original Excerpt

> "From Stettin in the Baltic to Trieste in the Adriatic, an iron curtain has descended across the Continent. Behind that line lie all the capitals of the ancient states of Central and Eastern Europe. Warsaw, Berlin, Prague, Vienna, Budapest, Belgrade, Bucharest and Sofia, all these famous cities and the populations around them lie in what I must call the Soviet sphere."

## Summary

Churchill delivered this speech at Westminster College in Fulton, Missouri, with U.S. President Harry S. Truman in attendance. It is widely considered the opening salvo of the Cold War. Churchill called for Anglo-American unity against Soviet expansionism and warned of the threat posed by communist totalitarianism.

## Political Significance

- Marked the beginning of the Cold War rhetoric
- Defined Churchill's post-war ideological stance: staunch anti-communism
- Established the concept of the "special relationship" between the UK and US
- Shaped Western foreign policy for the next four decades
