## Original Excerpt

> "What General Weygand called the Battle of France is over. I expect that the Battle of Britain is about to begin. Upon this battle depends the survival of Christian civilization. Upon it depends our own British life, and the long continuity of our institutions and our Empire. The whole fury and might of the enemy must very soon be turned on us. Hitler knows that he will have to break us in this island or lose the war. If we can stand up to him, all Europe may be free and the life of the world may move forward into broad, sunlit uplands. But if we fail, then the whole world, including the United States, including all that we have known and cared for, will sink into the abyss of a new Dark Age made more sinister, and perhaps more protracted, by the lights of perverted science. Let us therefore brace ourselves to our duties, and so bear ourselves that, if the British Empire and its Commonwealth last for a thousand years, men will still say, 'This was their finest hour.'"

## Summary

Delivered to the House of Commons just days after the fall of France, this speech rallied the British people during their darkest hour. Churchill refused to consider defeat or surrender, framing the battle against Nazi Germany as a fight for civilization itself.

## Political Significance

- Defined Churchill's wartime leadership style: defiant, inspirational, uncompromising
- Established "never surrender" as a core principle of his political identity
- Demonstrated his rhetorical power to mobilize a nation
- Set the stage for the Anglo-American alliance
