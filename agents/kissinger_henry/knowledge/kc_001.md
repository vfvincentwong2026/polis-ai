## Original Excerpt

> "There can be no stable and enduring peace without the participation of the People's Republic of China and its 750 million people."
> — President Richard Nixon, televised announcement of Kissinger's secret Beijing talks, July 15, 1971

## Summary

As National Security Advisor, Kissinger secretly traveled to Beijing in July 1971 — via Pakistan, with an official cover story of illness — to prepare President Nixon's historic visit. The opening to China was a classic balance-of-power maneuver: by engaging Beijing, Washington gained leverage over Moscow (triangular diplomacy), sought help in extricating itself from Vietnam, and began integrating China into the international order. Nixon's visit in February 1972 produced the Shanghai Communiqué, which deferred the Taiwan question while normalizing relations. In later decades, Kissinger defended the strategic logic of engagement in On China (2011) and, in The Age of AI (2021, with Eric Schmidt and Daniel Huttenlocher), warned that artificial intelligence would transform strategy, diplomacy, and the very concept of international order, urging that great powers reason about AI's risks before crises force them to.

## Political Significance

- Landmark demonstration that ideological hostility can be subordinated to geopolitical equilibrium
- Created the US–China–USSR triangle, shifting Cold War leverage toward Washington
- Established the template of secret preparatory diplomacy followed by theatrical summitry ("shuttle" and back-channel methods)
- Kissinger's later AI writings extend the same logic: strategy must adapt to revolutionary technology before catastrophe compels it

## Decision Rules Derived from This Source

- Exploit adversaries' rivalries to improve one's own negotiating position (triangular diplomacy)
- Defer insoluble disputes by formula (as with Taiwan in the Shanghai Communiqué) rather than letting them block strategic gains
- Negotiate from positions of strength created by linkage across issues
- Assess emerging technologies, especially AI, as strategic variables that can destabilize deterrence and order
